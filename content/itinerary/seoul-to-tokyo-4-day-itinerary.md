## The choices behind this itinerary

**One day for Harajuku and Omotesando, another for Asakusa and Ueno.** This 3-night plan is for cafes, shopping and contrasting neighborhoods, not a checklist that also requires Shibuya, Shinjuku and Ginza.

Choose one main place and one optional stop for each full day. Leave the rest for meals, transport and rest. If a theme park or specific exhibition is your top priority, replace a whole day rather than adding it on.

This page is planning guidance only. It does not include real-time prices, live availability, visa rules, opening hours, safety guarantees, or booking guarantees. For a personalized destination shortlist, start with the [TripCompass AI finder](/#destination-finder).

## Suggested trip length and daily route

**4 days and 3 nights: travel-focused arrival and departure, neighborhood exploration on Days 2 and 3.** Reorder the days for your actual terminal, arrival time and hotel location.

### Day 1: Dinner in the hotel area

Travel from the airport, settle in and eat nearby. After a late arrival, avoid shopping or observation-deck reservations. Check the airport name, terminal, onward transport and late check-in separately.

Even with an early arrival, use spare time to get familiar with the hotel area and rest rather than visiting every store planned for tomorrow.

### Day 2: Harajuku toward Omotesando

1. **Morning:** choose one store or street in Harajuku that matters to you. Do not assume stores will be open when you arrive.
2. **Lunch and afternoon:** group the next part toward Omotesando, leaving time for lunch and a cafe. Check current opening arrangements and queues before choosing.
3. **Late afternoon:** see one additional store or return to rest. Remove another stop if shopping takes longer than expected.

[GO TOKYO's Aoyama and Omotesando guide](https://www.gotokyo.org/en/destinations/western-tokyo/aoyama-and-omotesando/index.html) provides context for shopping, streets and architecture. Actual walking routes depend on the stores selected; this is not a guarantee of an effortless route.

**Left out:** adding Shinjuku night views and Ginza shopping to the same day. If Shibuya matters most, replace Harajuku or Omotesando with it instead of requiring all three.

### Day 3: Asakusa, with Ueno if energy allows

**Start in Asakusa; choose Ueno or more time in Asakusa after lunch.** Check a public transport connection between the areas rather than assuming a continuous walking route.

1. Pick one anchor in Asakusa and leave time for food and exploring nearby.
2. If energy allows after lunch, travel to Ueno for the park area or one exhibition that interests you.
3. If it rains or an exhibition deserves more time, remove another stop. Check museum opening dates, exhibitions and admission conditions first.

[GO TOKYO's travel ideas](https://www.gotokyo.org/en/see-and-do/index.html) provide area and interest context. If Akihabara matters more, replace Ueno. Do not add Ueno, Akihabara and Ginza as further requirements.

### Day 4: Finish around airport travel

Set aside check-out, luggage collection, the airport journey and the check-in allowance advised by your airline. If departing from a different airport, do not reuse the arrival route without checking.

Add a meal or final purchase near the hotel only when you have confirmed spare time. Do not make another reservation on the assumption that departure-day sightseeing time is guaranteed.

Use the [arrival and departure day guide](/itinerary/japan-arrival-departure-day-plan/) to separate Haneda/Narita checks, confirm bag collection and decide what to drop if the plan changes.

## Choosing a hotel area

| Area to compare | Priority it supports | Before booking |

| Access to Shibuya and Harajuku | A longer cafe and shopping day on Day 2 | Actual station and exit, room total and return route |
| Around Ueno | A stronger focus on the eastern day | Routes from both airports and luggage movement |

A familiar station name does not make both sightseeing days nearby. Mark two priority places and the hotel on a map, then compare return journeys. Assess all travelers, rooms and luggage rather than the room rate alone.

## If it rains or walking becomes tiring

- **Rain on Day 2:** visit fewer stores and stay with indoor shops and cafes in the current area. Keep alternatives to restaurants with long queues.
- **Rain on Day 3:** center the day on one Ueno exhibition only after checking it is operating and admission is possible. Otherwise stay near the hotel with an indoor plan.
- **Tired feet:** choose Asakusa or Ueno rather than both, or drop additional shopping on Day 2. Rest is separate from travel time.

Indoor alternatives can also require reservations or close. Reconsider travel itself during severe weather.

## Budget considerations

Tokyo budgets depend heavily on hotel area and daily movement. A room near your planned neighborhoods can reduce wasted time. A cheaper hotel farther out may be fine if transit is simple, but it can become tiring when the itinerary crosses the city.

Before booking, compare [Tokyo vs Osaka](/compare/tokyo-vs-osaka/), [best Japan cities for first-time travelers from Korea](/from-seoul/best-japan-cities-first-time-travelers/), and [Japan travel budget from Seoul](/from-seoul/japan-travel-budget/).

Start with flights, three hotel nights and airport transfers, then add local transport, meals, connectivity, optional admissions and a contingency. **Give shopping a separate cap rather than mixing it with essential costs.** Include repeat cafe visits in the food budget. Do not deduct discounts or pass benefits that you have not checked.
